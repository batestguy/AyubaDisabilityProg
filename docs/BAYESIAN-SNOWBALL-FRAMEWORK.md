## Research basis

Snowball recruitment grows through participants' contacts. Respondent-driven sampling (RDS) is a more specified chain-referral design and analysis approach; an ordinary referral registry must not be called a representative RDS survey simply because it records referral chains. Seed selection, network connections and referral behaviour can affect who is reached. See [Gile and Handcock, Respondent-Driven Sampling: An Assessment of Current Methodology](https://handcock.github.io/publication/assess-rds/).

Handcock, Gile and Mar's [Estimating hidden population size using Respondent-Driven Sampling data](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4500293/) develops Bayesian population-size estimation using a successive-sampling approximation and ordered personal-network-size observations. It is an analytical method with data and modelling assumptions, not an automatic way to populate a database. Its use for this programme remains a question for the later protocol; no population size, recruitment target, prior or representativeness claim is chosen now.

[Johnston et al.'s review of RDS surveys](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/79773) identifies reporting of seeds, recruitment waves and network-size adjustment as important to assessing survey quality. These findings motivate making recruitment records and analysis design explicit if a formal sampling study is subsequently approved.

## Questions reserved for the protocol

- Define the database's purpose, geography and eligibility for each group, allowing overlapping roles where appropriate. Distinguish voluntary programme registration from research participation and public-directory consent.
- Agree seed diversity and accessible direct-entry routes with disability organisations. Include people with weak networks or limited connectivity; ability to recruit others must not become a condition of training access.
- Agree voluntary invitations and data minimisation. Participants should not upload another person's details without permission. Referral links should remain private operational/research records, not public social graphs.
- Decide whether the objective is a registry or estimation of population characteristics/coverage. If formal RDS/Bayesian inference is appropriate, define the referral protocol, recruitment order/waves, network-size questions, nonresponse records, priors, diagnostics and sensitivity checks before collection.
- Define coverage review, duplicate resolution, retention/withdrawal and reporting that distinguishes observed registrations from model estimates and uncertainty.

These are open design questions. The placeholder has no forms, live recruitment controls or statistical results.
