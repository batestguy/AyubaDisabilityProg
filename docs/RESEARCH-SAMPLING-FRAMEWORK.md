## Changes to the six-part outline

1. Define the eligible population, pilot geography and expansion criteria.
2. Reach diverse communities through several accessible channels and voluntary invitations.
3. Maintain consented, deduplicated records with separate optional research participation.
4. Review facilitators, identify learning barriers and match online teaching capacity.
5. Design a separate research sample with a defined covered population and justified sample size.
6. Monitor learning delivery, attrition, missingness and people not yet reached.

The methodological choice is a proposal informed by the linked guidance, not a claim that those sources endorse this project. Registration and purposive/referral recruitment are not equivalent to a national probability sample. Sampling only database members cannot establish national coverage. No sample size, population denominator or completed partnership is invented. Bayesian analysis is no longer presented as a required stage or as a remedy for recruitment bias.

## Decisions before implementation

Agree eligibility and age policy, pilot locations, community partners, database stewardship, consent materials, fields, security and retention arrangements. Define study outcomes, target population, usable sampling frame, precision, reporting groups, expected response and field budget before calculating a sample size. Co-design accessible collection and online teaching with people with disabilities.

This change is research-page content only. It does not collect personal data, create a backend, change demonstration records or connect to government systems.

## Statistical table and adaptive recruitment

The page now keeps an eight-stage table and six-part framework expanded. Each table row pairs a statistical method with an illustrative SVG, everyday explanation, proposed pilot timing and readiness condition. Timing is a project planning estimate; national expansion is open-ended. Short finite animations respect OS and site reduced-motion settings. Mobile readers can scroll the semantic table horizontally using touch or keyboard.

The recruitment stage explicitly proposes **adaptive snowball recruitment** with purposively varied starting contacts. Ordinary snowball sampling is documented by the linked UNHCR guidance; weekly coverage reviews and changes to starting contacts/channels are this project's proposed operational adaptation. This is not formal adaptive cluster sampling or respondent-driven sampling. Inclusion probabilities remain unknown. The pilot must establish review triggers; staff should record changes and use non-referral outreach for people outside existing networks. Apparent referral saturation does not prove population coverage.

The research sample remains separate. Formula notation and assumptions are explained beside the table, with no invented national sample target. The delivery rows cover facilitator capacity, matching and cohort follow-up rather than treating enrolment as a learning outcome.
