Evaluate trainer expertise, safe delivery, equipment, local demand and learner preferences before selecting them. Tool-intensive or chemical/heat/electrical work needs supervised provision; this does not mean excluding a learner based on disability.

## Starter course outlines

Each proposed starter follows six short text-first lessons, quiz retries, a practical assignment, trainer feedback and accessible learning materials. Exact technical content needs a competent vocational reviewer before publication.

| Lesson | Sewing/textiles | Small-space growing | Retail/customer service |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Choose a product and organise an accessible workspace | Choose a growing goal and accessible site | Understand the customer and service task |
| 2 | Recognise materials and plan tool/support needs | Identify locally suitable plants and necessary resources | Communicate clearly and accessibly |
| 3 | Measure and make a paper pattern | Plan containers/beds and an observation record | Count stock and record a fictional order |
| 4 | Plan and observe joining/finishing methods | Observe supervised planting/care routines | Check quantities, prices and change using fictional totals |
| 5 | Inspect quality and estimate material costs | Identify what observations to discuss with an extension adviser | Respond to a complaint and protect customer information |
| 6 | Prepare a sample product brief and customer offer | Review a practice log and a small growing budget | Complete a role play and a simple service portfolio example |

Existing Business Foundations supplies budgeting/customer-offer support; Spreadsheet Skills supports records; Digital Essentials supports communication; AI Essentials can help draft fictional plans with human checking. Avoid requiring all four as prerequisites. A learner may begin a practical course directly and use a supporting module when useful.

## Journey, evidence and completion

Choose interest → inspect delivery mode/tools/access options → learn basics → prepare practice plan → obtain support or supervised practice when needed → submit evidence → trainer reviews → revise → receive the correctly labelled record → add an assessed portfolio example → choose employment/business/teaching next steps.

Proposed presentation distinguishes:

- **Foundation learning:** text lessons, quiz and a reviewed plan/role play. A demo achievement establishes completion of those tasks, not competence in operating a machine, producing food or performing a trade.
- **Practical evidence submitted:** a description/photo/PDF or observation note awaiting review. Preserve existing local attachment limits; a self-uploaded photo is a claim until reviewed. Do not require video or identifiable photos.
- **Trainer-observed practice:** a dated task-specific rubric records what was observed, the assessor and feedback. In the current demo it must be labelled sample/simulated observation. Real competence assessment would need a qualified assessor and actual practice outside the app.

Do not award an overall trade-competence record solely for lessons and a quiz. When a course's configured task requires observation, trainer observation is an additional gate beyond the current all-lessons/quiz ≥70/submitted-work/approval rules. Keep the original four courses' rules unchanged. Resubmission clears approval for the changed task; retained reviewed versions and portfolios must not imply current approval of newer work.

For initial delivery, publish complete foundation courses only after content review. Do not display a supervised workshop as available until its simulated/real availability is clearly identified. A learner without tools can finish foundation learning and request support while hands-on practice remains pending; do not treat a written plan as completed making/growing practice.

## Accessibility and support

Keep all courses open regardless of disability/gender. Ask about task-specific preferences and support without collecting medical evidence. Offer large-print diagrams, text alternatives to demonstrations, captions/transcripts for any later video, accessible work heights/tool grips where appropriate, flexible pacing, reachable materials and alternative ways to submit evidence. Confirm adaptations with the learner and trainer; do not infer a person's capacity from a category.

Add tools/materials, assistive adaptations, workspace, transport, power/water and local trainer access to the existing support checklist. Record what is available and what is needed. Support requests remain pending; do not promise equipment, grants, apprenticeship places or jobs. Business creation is one option alongside employment, apprenticeship, personal development and eventual teaching.

## Administrator and Facilitator implications

- Course records need subject/category, delivery mode (app foundation / supported practice / supervised workshop), tools/materials, reviewer expertise, accessibility options and a task-specific assessment rubric.
- Administrator publishing checks verify those fields and retain existing course versions. Proposed partner/workshop records must distinguish sample entries, verified provision and expired/unverified information; no external enrolment is created.
- Administrator coordinates support, equipment needs and follow-up. Facilitators assess task evidence and observed practice; administrative approval of support cannot satisfy learning assessment.
- Trainer applications must identify what a person can teach and the relevant trade experience/evidence. Completion of an introductory app course alone must not imply qualification to supervise specialist tasks.
- Reports distinguish foundation completions, submitted practice, trainer-observed practice and support needs. Never convert them into funded equipment, employment, income or accredited qualifications.
