## Proposed Nigerian study structure

This is a recommendation requiring protocol development, disability-community involvement, sampling expertise, funding and approval. No sample size, state allocation, cost or collection date is agreed.

### Voluntary learner/participant registry

Offer accessible registration through community partnerships, direct entry, assisted/offline participation and monitored referrals. Include routes for people outside strong networks and without smartphones. Referral participation must be voluntary and must not determine access to training. Invite people to contact the project themselves; do not upload another person's details without permission. Track recruitment source, accessibility needs, broad coverage gaps and consented referral metadata; keep social graphs private. Define duplicate review and eligibility checks before recruitment, including how uncertain matches are resolved without excluding people who share a phone.

Registry counts describe registered participants and operational needs. An open registry has no known sampling denominator and cannot measure national prevalence, state shares or the total unregistered population by subtraction from an unverified total.

### Separate facilitator register

Assemble candidates from several provider and practitioner sources, such as training institutions, disability organisations, professional networks and direct applications. These are proposed source categories, not confirmed partnerships. Verify identity, teaching scope, relevant experience, accessible teaching capability and availability through an agreed process. Keep private verification records separate from optional public listings. Public-listing consent and content review must precede publication; inclusion is not an unqualified endorsement or proof of available national capacity.

### Independent probability survey

Propose a stratified, multistage, **two-phase household survey spanning all 37 locations**, independent of whether people entered the registry. Select areas and households from an approved frame with known conditional selection probabilities. Phase one uses standard functioning questions; phase two samples for detailed needs questions, oversampling relevant functioning groups if justified while also including some people **below the functioning threshold** to investigate screening misses. Document the phase-two selection rule and probabilities for both groups. Screening questions do not constitute a clinical diagnosis.

Use standard functioning measures alongside separate questions on training demand, existing skills, prior provision, assistive technology, connectivity and access barriers. Select and test instruments, translations and accessible administration modes before field use; do not quietly rewrite standard items and retain claims of equivalence. A below-threshold subsample can investigate misses, but cannot alone prove the screen's clinical sensitivity without a justified reference assessment.

Document household-frame exclusions explicitly: institutional residents, people without stable housing, inaccessible/insecure areas and others outside the selected frame may require separate coverage arrangements. Do not describe a private-household result as covering everyone. Record contact attempts, refusal, noncontact and item nonresponse; do not substitute convenient households silently.

Keep distinct consent choices for **survey participation, registration, follow-up, record linkage and public listing**. Refusal of registration or listing must not prevent eligible survey participation. Specify linkage accuracy, permitted uses, withdrawal, access controls and retention arrangements before collection. The demo's local storage and sign-in model are not the research data infrastructure.

### Analysis and denominators

Publish design-weighted findings first, with uncertainty that accounts for stratification, clusters, both phases and nonresponse. Describe coverage and missing-data limitations. Bayesian analysis is optional only under a justified likelihood, priors and selection model, with diagnostics, sensitivity checks and appropriate external benchmarks. It does not repair unknown selection by itself.

| Question | Required denominator / scope |
|---|---|
| How many eligible people are unregistered? | Eligible population in the survey frame, with a defined registry, reference date and consented linkage or clearly defined self-report. Distinguish national disability registration from project registration. Registry totals alone cannot answer this. |
| Who has unmet training needs? | Defined eligible population with an explicitly measured training need and a specified unmet-need rule/time period. Do not substitute all registered people or all people with disabilities without explanation. |
| Where are facilitator-capacity gaps? | Required teaching capacity for specified courses, access modes, geography and period compared with verified available capacity in compatible units. Headcounts alone are not teaching hours, places or accessible delivery capacity. |

Pooled pilot results should primarily inform feasibility and instrument/recruitment quality. State tables must show effective information and uncertainty; suppress or qualify unsupported estimates. Geographic presence does not imply adequate state precision.

## Mathematics and presentation specification

The future page/presentation must include the following **four-column table**, with readable notation and a plain-language equivalent. Here, `S` is the observed sample, `y_i` an outcome indicator, `d_i` eligible personal-network degree, `U` the target population and `S_(t-1)` the units already selected. Equations are method descriptions, not implemented analysis.

| Method | Mathematics/statistics | Simple explanation | Diagram/animation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary snowball recruitment | `p_observed = sum_(i in S) y_i / n`; inclusion probabilities generally unknown. Referral expansion is a process, not a probability estimator. | People invite contacts. Well-connected groups may dominate and disconnected groups can remain unseen. | Reveal fictional seeds and waves; retain an unreachable group outside every chain. |
| Adaptive outreach | Example operational gap: `g_k = max(0, T_k - n_k)`, with chosen target `T_k` and observed count `n_k`. No implied population weight. | Review who is missing and change outreach channels or assistance. Targets are operational goals, not population shares. | Show a gap prompting assisted/offline outreach; distinguish a target from a census benchmark. |
| RDS-II / Volz–Heckathorn | `p_VH = [sum_(i in S) y_i/d_i] / [sum_(i in S) 1/d_i]`, for positive, credible degrees. Needs strong network/recruitment assumptions, sufficient mixing and a suitable small sampling-fraction approximation. | People with more eligible contacts can be easier to reach; inverse-degree weighting changes their relative contribution. It cannot reveal a disconnected population automatically. | Compare equal contributions with inverse-degree contributions and show sensitivity to degree errors. |
| Successive sampling | Working next-draw model: `P(J_t=i given S_(t-1), d_U) = d_i / sum_(j in U minus S_(t-1)) d_j` for an unselected unit. Overall `pi_i = P(i in S)` requires the full sampling process; the displayed conditional probability is **not** `pi_i`. | Remove each selected person before the next draw. The remaining pool changes; estimating inclusion needs more than one draw's probability. | Remove fictional nodes after selection; distinguish next-draw chances from overall inclusion. |
| Bayesian updating | `p(theta given D,M) proportional to p(D given theta,M) p(theta given M)`. A population-size model includes `N` and degree/selection assumptions in `theta,M`. | Combine a stated model of observed data with prior knowledge. Different plausible priors or selection assumptions can change results. | Show prior, likelihood and posterior; switch assumptions and display uncertainty intervals. |
| Independent probability survey | For a selected person on one nested path: `pi_i = p_area × p_household_given_area × p_person_given_household × p_phase2_given_phase1`; include further selection stages if used. Initial weight `w_i = 1/pi_i`; ratio `p_hat = sum(w_i y_i)/sum(w_i)`. | Randomly select from a defined frame, record every selection chance and account for clustering and both phases. | Animate strata → areas → households → screening → phase-two selection, with some below-threshold participants retained. |

For a census of eligible people within a selected household, the person-selection factor is one. The product expression requires the actual design's nested path; designs with multiple selection paths need their correct overall probabilities. **Nonresponse adjustment is separate from initial selection weights**: a later factor (for example `1/r_hat_i`) requires its own response model/classes and checks. Calibration, if justified, is another explicit step. Use design-aware variance estimation, not independent-binomial intervals for clustered data. No national weights have been selected.

### Checked hypothetical example

**Hypothetical illustration** — fictional degrees `[10,10,2,2]`, outcomes `[1,1,0,1]`:

`p_observed = (1+1+0+1)/4 = 75%`.

`p_inverse_degree = (1/10 + 1/10 + 0/2 + 1/2) / (1/10 + 1/10 + 1/2 + 1/2) = 0.7/1.2 = 58.3%` (rounded).
