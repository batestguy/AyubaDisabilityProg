## Agreed purpose and framing

The owner selected **a Plateau pilot partnership and technical scoping** as the main presentation outcome. The research should appreciate Gufwan’s existing work, build on his recruitment approach, and show how statistical sampling methods could strengthen recruitment.

Proposed central message:

> Your work has created valuable community connections and practical ways of reaching people. We propose to build on that experience, using statistical sampling methods to plan recruitment, learn from each outreach round and connect more people with accessible learning.

Gufwan’s experience and community relationships are substantive contributions to joint research. The statisticians would help formalise, test and extend that knowledge. Agree practical and academic roles together.

## Show how statistical methods change recruitment

The benefits below are hypotheses for local testing. Register growth, participant diversity and population-representative estimates are different objectives.

| Method to investigate | Addition to existing outreach | Recruitment benefit to test | Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stratified selection of starting contacts | Use his team’s knowledge to choose varied contacts across agreed areas and priority groups | Broader participation from the first round; less concentration in one network | Defined groups/scope and contact selection records. Purposive seed selection alone is not probability sampling. |
| Adaptive snowball recruitment | Retain trusted referrals; review each wave and add contacts or routes where agreed groups have low participation | Additional unique registrations and improved inclusion | Consented source/wave codes, dates, prespecified review rules and an adaptation log; accessible direct entry remains available. |
| Venue-and-time sampling or recruitment | Add agreed visits to relevant community settings at selected times | Participation from people reached less often through digital or association routes | Venue/time list and an invitation rule. Formal selection probabilities require a sufficiently defined design; venue attendees are not the entire population. |
| Adaptive allocation, with a Bayesian option | Use early rounds to update knowledge about each route’s reach and direct later effort | Inclusion per staff hour or naira while retaining effort for people requiring more support | Comparable effort/cost records, uncertainty estimates, human review and minimum-effort safeguards. Exploratory local adaptation, not an established Nigerian result. |
| Stratified probability selection for an optional study | Select research participants from an agreed, checked frame for specific questions | Defensible comparisons and stated precision for the defined covered population | Eligible frame, allocation, known selection probabilities, response records and design-aware analysis. Wider coverage may need an independent area/household frame. |
| Formal respondent-driven sampling, if suitable | Specify invitations and collect appropriate network information in a linked recruitment study | Investigate network reach and model-based estimation options | Network connection, credible degree/composition information, protocol, diagnostics and sensitivity analysis. Assess suitability before adopting the label. |

Recommended starting proposal: planned starting contacts, monitored referral waves and complementary accessible routes. Use the pilot to assess model-assisted allocation and formal network-sampling feasibility. Design any probability research sample for a defined target and frame. The prospective academic contribution is the evaluated local adaptation of these established or emerging methods.

## How the pilot would demonstrate improvement

Document the current procedure jointly. Scope three comparison strategies: (A) current outreach with common recording; (B) outreach with planned starting-contact diversity; (C) outreach with agreed adaptive changes. They are proposed variants, not instructions to discontinue ongoing services.

Compare like observation periods and agreed effort or budgets. If feasible, jointly design random allocation across appropriate recruitment units, accounting for cross-referrals, spillover and independent units when planning power and analysis. Otherwise report feasibility and descriptive differences with uncertainty and context. Sequential waves alone cannot establish a causal improvement.

| Proposed measure | Definition or interpretation |
|---|---|
| Unique eligible registrations | Consented people meeting agreed eligibility/verification checks; pending duplicate reviews separate |
| Recruitment rate | Unique eligible registrations / recorded staff hours for the same strategy and period |
| Recruitment cost | Attributable cost / unique eligible registrations, using agreed cost allocation |
| Group inclusion | Counts and composition across agreed groups; retain unknowns and account for overlapping categories |
| Invitation outcomes | Contacted, eligible, consented, completed, declined and unreachable where denominators are measurable; unobserved invitations cannot support a response rate |
| Recruitment depth and route | Unique additions by route and referral wave, with overlaps reconciled and source codes private |
| Participation burden | Completion time, requested accommodations and voluntary feedback, with defined denominators |
| Learning connection | Requests → matched → started → assessed skills for specified cohorts; waiting and missing follow-ups visible |

Use population-coverage percentages only with defensible denominators and frames. Operational targets are not automatically population proportions. Access to services must not depend on recruiting others or joining research.

## Evidence informing the proposal

- **Adaptive disability outreach:** Johansson, Gulliksen and Gustavsson used monitored adaptive snowball recruitment and accessible collection modes in Sweden. This is a precedent to adapt and test, not Nigerian performance evidence. [Methods §§2.1–2.2, online publication 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-020-00714-x).
- **Complementary routes:** Stöcker and Zindel’s 2026 German comparison reached different participant groups through online, organisation and workshop venue recruitment. It supports local route comparisons, not a claim that mixed routes alone create a probability sample. [Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-026-01376-x).
- **Modern allocation:** Borza and colleagues evaluated computational allocation, including a Thompson-sampling baseline, in U.S.-based simulations using clinical-network data. Representation measures depended on known target distributions. This motivates exploratory modelling, not claims of field gains in Plateau or importing U.S. demographic targets. [AMIA proceedings, available 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12099364/).
- **Formal adaptive designs:** Thompson explains adaptive network/spatial selection under explicitly specified designs. These are distinct from simply changing outreach after a weekly review. [Survey Methodology, 2011](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-001-x/2011002/article/11607-eng.pdf).
- **RDS field lessons:** ONS’s 2026 smartphone tests found feasibility and estimate accuracy varied across populations and characteristics. Assess access modes, network information and validation locally. [ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/methodologicalpublications/generalmethodology/onsworkingpaperseries/testingsmartphonebasedrespondentdrivensampling).
- **Bayesian size estimation:** Handcock, Gile and Mar use ordered network-size information under a successive-sampling approximation. Size estimation is a separate task from recruiting people; retain it as a conditional research option with sensitivity analysis. [Electronic Journal of Statistics, 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4500293/).
- **Frames and inference:** World Bank LSMS guidance supports a suitable frame, design, sample size and weights for a defined population. [Sampling, Weighting & Estimation](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/lsms/sampling).
- **Instruments:** Review existing questions jointly, then assess appropriate functioning questions and separately ask about learning/access needs. Age scope, translations and administration require agreement. [Official Washington Group sets](https://www.washingtongroup-disability.com/question-sets/).
- **Teaching:** CAST’s engagement, representation and action/expression principles support showing concrete accessibility options. [CAST](https://www.cast.org/resources/about-universal-design-for-learning/).

## What makes the presentation convincing

Show the existing pathway, then add one statistical feature at a time: planned contacts, a feedback loop, complementary routes and measured allocation. End with how the pilot would evaluate those additions.

Use real credited photographs, an illustrative recruitment animation, a labelled planning matrix, one accessible lesson and the prototype journey. Keep detailed estimators, formulas and literature comparisons in the reading edition. Do not present fabricated participants, numerical gains, budgets, testimonials or maps as observations.

Scoping questions: Which channels, referral rules, eligibility/needs assessments, records and monitoring practices already exist? What evidence would his team value? Which additions are feasible? Discuss institutional alignment only after agreeing definitions, permissions and stewardship.
