4. Building on your work: one database, an adaptive cycle

  • Your community relationships, outreach and practical support
  • Proposed extension: Designed together
  • Adaptive snowball recruitment: Invite voluntary participant referrals through trusted connections. Use recorded recruitment evidence to adjust the next round, investigate gaps and decide where to focus effort.
  • Understand needs: Review recorded skills, interests and access needs with participants to understand the learning and support that would suit them.
  • Match teaching: Use recorded interests and learning needs to identify suitable teaching. The learner and facilitator agree the arrangement.
  • Support practice: Use the agreed learning plan and support needs to guide accessible teaching, practical tasks and feedback.
  • Monitor & review: Record learning progress and recruitment evidence in the database. Use progress to adjust support and recruitment evidence to refine outreach.
  • Participant database: Keep recruitment details, skills, learning needs, agreed support and progress together. These records inform decisions throughout the cycle.

5. Adaptive snowball recruitment

  • Building on your community connections
  • Your relationships already connect people to support.
  • Who are we missing—and how can we reach them?
  • Each round shows who our networks reach — so we can go looking for who they miss.
  • Round 1: Start with varied trusted contacts. A few starting contacts, spread across different communities.
  • Round 2: People invite others they know — if they choose. Referrals spread through personal networks. Some groups are not reached yet.
  • Round 3: Check who's missing, change where we look. Add a new starting contact inside the group not yet reached, or use another route.
  • Next round: start again from Round 1 with what we learned.
  • Key: Starting contact; Reached through a referral; Not reached yet
  • Referrals make it snowball.
  • Checking and changing course makes it adaptive.
  • People with few connections may need other routes.
  • How we learn from each round (proposed Bayesian refinement): p(θ | D) ∝ p(D | θ) p(θ)
  • How each round helps us learn: Start with your team's community knowledge. Record who joins, through which route, and how much outreach effort was needed. Update what we expect from each route—and how uncertain we are. Use that evidence to agree the next round, keeping attention on harder-to-reach groups.
  • What the symbols mean: θ = recruitment rates being estimated. D = recruitment records. p(θ) = starting expectations. p(D | θ) = how well each possible rate explains the records under the model. p(θ | D) = updated understanding. ∝ = “is proportional to”.
  • Proposed model · Decisions remain with the team
  • Potential gaps to assess together: People not yet reached; Groups reached less often; Access barriers

Proposed pilot method for Nigeria, not an existing fitted model or observed recruitment result. Confirm current referral, recording and adaptation practices with Gufwan and his team; do not assume these are missing. Complementary routes remain important. Bayesian updating is an optional proposed refinement, not a requirement of snowball recruitment. Define the likelihood, priors, route attribution, dependence, effort and safeguards jointly before fitting a model. Review uncertainty, inclusion and access alongside recruitment efficiency. No probability sample or population representativeness is established. Referral-based recruitment over-represents well-connected people and people similar to the starting contacts, and can miss isolated people; changing starting contacts and routes between rounds reduces, but does not remove, this bias. This is not respondent-driven sampling. Referrals and research are voluntary and do not determine access to learning/support. Documentary photograph shows mobility devices at a historical partnership activity, not recruitment or proposed pilot activity.

Documented community outreach: reported interviews, July 2026

Swedish adaptive snowball sampling precedent: 771 respondents, 35 diagnoses/impairments; feasibility, not proof of representative coverage

Adapting snowball recruitment for hard-to-reach groups: Sadler et al., 2010, Nursing & Health Sciences

Limits of referral-based sampling for hidden populations: Magnani et al., 2005, AIDS

Bayesian recruitment allocation research: proposed modelling context, not Nigerian field evidence

6. A participant database that guides learning and support

  • Building on the need you have already identified
  • Ayuba Gufwan, TheCable interview, 15 November 2025: “In the long term, we plan to build a comprehensive, disaggregated database of PWDs in Nigeria.”
  • Our proposed contribution for Nigeria
  • Learners: Adaptive snowball recruitment
  • Facilitators: Volunteering or recruitment
  • Recruitment records populate the participant database from the first recruitment round. Record location and recruitment route alongside the participant-agreed profile.
  • Participant database: Learner skills, interests & goals; Learner access & support needs; Facilitator skills & availability; Agreed pathways & progress
  • Records inform decisions with participants and facilitators: Understand needs; Match teaching; Plan support together; Review progress
  • Facilitator records describe teaching skills and areas, availability and whether they join through volunteering or recruitment. Agree teaching scope before matching.
  • Plan support together means participants, facilitators and the team agree what help is needed, who will arrange it and when. A plan is not confirmation that support has been delivered.
  • Update records as goals, support and progress change.
  • Proposed participant database for Nigeria · Not the official national database

TheCable interview published 15 November 2025 records Gufwan’s national database ambition and describes consultants/data collection already underway at that time. This is dated interview context, not a claim about the current status of national data. Our independent proposed participant database for Nigeria is a scoped learning/support contribution, not the national database, a census, a complete population register or an agreed official integration/partnership. Records begin during recruitment and are updated with learners and facilitators throughout the learning cycle. Facilitators can join through volunteering or recruitment; teaching scope, skills and availability are reviewed before matching. These are proposed profile categories, not new accounts, approved appointments or delivered services. Collect only agreed necessary information, keep optional sensitive/research disclosures separate from access to services, and agree consent, stewardship and role-based access before real collection. Diagram shows proposed categories and human decisions, not real records, numerical outcomes or an automatic decision engine. The FMINO photograph is separate from the interview: it depicts Gufwan speaking in a leadership profile published 26 October 2024; no interview photo, event date or venue is inferred.

7. One cycle complete — the next one starts better informed

  • Each stage reaches a goal that meets a need
  • 1. Recruit: Reach people often missed
  • 2. Understand needs: Know each person's skills, goals and access needs
  • 3. Match teaching: The right skill with the right facilitator
  • 4. Support practice: Accessible learning that becomes real skill
  • 5. Monitor & review: Know what is working, and for whom
  • Participant database: Records what happened at every stage
  • The next round starts here: Progress evidence and Participant and facilitator feedback feed back from stage 5 into stage 1. What worked continues. What didn't, changes.
  • Every round meets a need, records what happened, and uses success and feedback to plan the next round.
  • Proposed cycle · No results yet · Measures agreed together during pilot design

Conceptual proposed pilot cycle for Nigeria, not an existing service, an agreed partnership or observed results. Stage goals describe intended needs met, not achieved outcomes. Progress evidence and participant and facilitator feedback inform the next round; how success is measured, who reviews it and how often are to be agreed with Gufwan’s team during pilot design. Repeating the cycle is programme improvement; individual participants are not required to stay. Recruitment changes between rounds follow the adaptive snowball approach on slide 5 and claim no representative coverage.
