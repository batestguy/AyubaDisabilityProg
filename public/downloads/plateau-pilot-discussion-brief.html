Objective

Two statisticians helping build a reliable database of people with disabilities and connect participants to accessible online teaching, building on Gufwan’s foundation.

Building on Gufwan’s foundation.

WHO’s historical profile connects Gufwan’s experience of polio with his work helping others access mobility and education. WHO: From neglect to inspiration.

Beautiful Gate reports more than 24,000 wheelchairs across 28 states, alongside other mobility and education support. These are its own cumulative figures without a reporting date. Beautiful Gate’s own account. A 2017 account describes production in Jos and outreach across 23 states at that time. World Renew: Entering through the Beautiful Gate. Historical reach is not a complete current register.

In his 2025 interview, Gufwan discusses disability data, education and access to services. These are attributed priorities and reported initiatives. TheCable: Interview with Ayuba Gufwan.

November 2026 reporting describes a workshop validating revised KPIs and NDMIS data collection tools, and a call for reliable disability data. Inclusive News Network: Disability data and NDMIS workshop. Possible alignment is a technical discussion; this report does not establish deployment, endorsement or an existing integration with our prototype.

Our contribution as two statisticians.

Jerry Bannister Zachary and Strong propose to contribute recruitment design, data quality checks, coverage monitoring and evaluation. We are two statisticians with a working prototype.

We propose to design varied recruitment channels, check duplicates and missing information, monitor who is being missed, and evaluate the pathway from registration to teaching and demonstrated skills.

Two statisticians helping build a reliable database of people with disabilities and connect participants to accessible online teaching, building on Gufwan’s foundation.

This is an independent proposal. Community access, facilitators, funding, secure operational infrastructure and accommodation support remain to be secured. No endorsement, delivery partnership or access to existing beneficiary records is assumed.

A proposed Plateau pilot.

Plateau is our proposed starting location for a limited pilot. Exact communities, eligibility, participant numbers and courses will be agreed during technical scoping.

Proposed activities: accessible voluntary registration, weekly coverage reviews, facilitator readiness checks, learner matching and supported teaching, followed by cohort review.

An approximately 20-week sequence is illustrative. The clock starts only after agreed readiness conditions: responsibilities, community permissions, consent and data arrangements, staffing, funding, secure collection and access support. Stages overlap and may take longer. Completion reviews depend on a full course cycle; registration and national expansion remain open-ended.

Three decisions for a first meeting

  1. Nominate a technical counterpart for a scoping discussion.
  2. Identify potential community introductions in Plateau, subject to their agreement.
  3. Agree pilot-scoping requirements: communities, eligibility, courses, capacity, data governance, costs and readiness.

Evidence outputs for review

Proposed evidence outputs: a checked register with a duplicate-review log; recruitment and coverage summaries with an outreach-change log; learner matching and waiting lists; cohort attendance and skills summaries; and a review of access barriers, participant feedback and costs. Share only agreed aggregate reporting, with small-group disclosure risks reviewed.

Agree measurement definitions, denominators and review frequency using the research plan. No results or targets are claimed.

Resources and preparation before recruitment.

This is an independent proposal. Community access, facilitators, funding, secure operational infrastructure and accommodation support remain to be secured. No endorsement, delivery partnership or access to existing beneficiary records is assumed.

  1. Co-design questions, consent and learning arrangements with people with disabilities.
  2. Review existing data tools and discuss possible NDMIS alignment with the responsible technical team; agree definitions, permissions and stewardship before any data sharing.
  3. Assess facilitator capacity, languages, devices, connectivity and accommodation barriers.
  4. Obtain local cost estimates for outreach, staffing, teaching, infrastructure and access support.
  5. Agree responsibilities, readiness criteria and a limited pilot protocol before recruitment.

Agree who provides each resource and who owns collection, safeguarding, teaching and follow-up. A working demonstration does not establish operational readiness.
